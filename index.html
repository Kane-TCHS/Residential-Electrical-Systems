Module 4: Residential Electrical Systems

Affordable Home Design · Design Portfolio · 25 points

Your house needs power in every room, put in the right places and done safely. In this module you'll lay out the electrical plan for your house using the same basic rules electricians follow.

Your house: It's all-electric. There's no gas and no garage.

1. How Power Gets In

Power comes from the utility line to a meter on an outside wall, which measures how much electricity you use. From there it goes to the main panel inside the house. The panel splits the power into circuits, each one protected by a breaker. A breaker shuts off its circuit if something goes wrong. The main breaker shuts off the whole house.

3. The Rules

Outlets

  • Walls: no spot along a wall can be more than 6′ from an outlet, so outlets go no more than 12′ apart. Any wall 2′ or wider gets one.
  • Kitchen counters: no spot on the counter can be more than 2′ from an outlet, so outlets go no more than 4′ apart.
  • Bathrooms: one outlet within 3′ of each sink.
  • Outdoors: one at the front of the house and one at the back.
  • Laundry: one outlet.
  • GFCI on kitchen counters, in bathrooms, in the laundry and outdoors. A GFCI cuts the power in a fraction of a second if current leaks, which matters most near water.

Lights and switches

  • Every room: a light controlled by a wall switch, bathrooms included.
  • Switch location: at the door, on the latch side (the side that opens, not the hinge side).
  • 3-way switches: a room or hall with two entrances gets a switch at each one, labeled S3.
  • Outside doors: a light at every one.
  • Bathrooms: an exhaust fan in every one, vented outside. It removes moisture that causes mold.
  • Switch lines: draw a dashed line from each switch to the light it controls.

Smoke alarms

  • One in every bedroom.
  • One in the hall outside the bedrooms.

Meter and panel

  • The meter goes on an outside wall.
  • The panel goes indoors, somewhere you can reach but out of sight, like a closet or the laundry.

4. Sketch Your Plan

  1. Print or trace your current floor plan. If you changed it after Module 1 feedback, use the new one.
  2. Write the length on every wall in every room.
  3. Draw all outlets, lights, switches, exhaust fans, smoke alarms, the meter and the panel, with labels.
  4. On kitchen counters, write the distance between outlets.
  5. Draw the switch lines dashed, in a second color if you have one.
  6. Copy the legend onto your sketch.
  7. Use dark pen, not pencil.

Photo rules

  • Shoot straight down, not at an angle.
  • The plan fills the frame, with all four edges showing.
  • Good light, no shadows or glare.
